# SUPERPOWER AI — Lead Follow-Up Pilot Sales Kit

## Offer statement

I help small service businesses make their lead follow-up process easier to track using a lightweight workflow, clear next actions, and human-reviewed message drafts.

This is an implementation offer hypothesis. Confirm scope and willingness to pay with each prospect before quoting or promising results.

## Who it may fit

- A team receiving enquiries through multiple channels.
- A business that lacks a consistent lead status or next-action owner.
- An owner willing to standardize the process.
- A business that can provide approved sample data and a decision-maker.

## Who it may not fit

- A business with very low enquiry volume and no meaningful follow-up problem.
- A buyer expecting guaranteed revenue growth.
- A team unwilling to define process ownership.
- A project requiring unsupported integrations, sensitive-data processing, or fully autonomous messaging before controls are designed.

## Discovery call structure (15–20 minutes)

1. Context (2 minutes): what does the business sell, and where do enquiries arrive?
2. Current workflow (5 minutes): how is each lead recorded, assigned, and followed up?
3. Failure points (4 minutes): where do delays, duplicates, or missed next actions happen?
4. Constraints (3 minutes): existing tools, permissions, data sensitivity, and team habits.
5. Success criteria (3 minutes): what would a useful pilot demonstrably improve?
6. Next step (2 minutes): decide together whether a scoped pilot is worth proposing.

Do not pressure a prospect who has no confirmed need.

## Objection responses

### “We already use a CRM.”
That may be the right solution already. I'd first look at whether the existing system can support the follow-up process before proposing another tool.

### “Can you guarantee more sales?”
No. I can agree on operational deliverables and measurable workflow criteria. Sales depend on multiple factors outside the automation itself.

### “Can we automate all messages?”
We should begin with drafts and human approval. Automated sending should only be added after permissions, consent, provider rules, error handling, and customer expectations are clear.

### “Why should we pay for a pilot?”
The pilot should be small enough to evaluate with explicit deliverables and acceptance criteria. If the expected value or scope is unclear, we should clarify it before proceeding.
