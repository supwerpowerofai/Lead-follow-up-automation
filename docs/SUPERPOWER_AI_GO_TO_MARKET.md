# SUPERPOWER AI — Go-to-Market Plan

> Earn attention with a clear promise. Earn trust with proof. Earn revenue by solving a real problem.

## Initial objective

Validate whether a narrowly scoped AI automation service solves a real business problem that a buyer is willing to pay to fix. Do not measure success by repository count, impressions, or likes alone.

## First customer segment

Start with small marketing agencies and service businesses that manage inbound enquiries. Interview both segments before committing to one niche. Choose based on observed pain, access to decision-makers, willingness to pay, and implementation complexity.

## Initial offer hypothesis: Lead Follow-Up Workflow Pilot

A small, clearly scoped implementation that helps a business track incoming leads, assign a next action, draft follow-up messages, and identify overdue follow-ups.

Candidate deliverables:
- Map the current process and pain points.
- Configure a tracker or lightweight workflow using tools the client already has.
- Provide human-reviewed message drafts rather than unapproved automated outreach.
- Test with client-approved sample data.
- Deliver a handover document and short walkthrough.
- Agree on a small measurement plan before implementation.

Explicit exclusions until separately agreed:
- Guaranteed sales or revenue increases.
- Unapproved sending of customer messages.
- Scraping private data or bypassing platform limits.
- Handling sensitive customer data without an agreed security and access plan.
- Unpriced integrations or unlimited ongoing support.

## Discovery before selling

Ask prospects:
1. Where do new enquiries arrive?
2. How are they assigned and tracked today?
3. What happens when a lead is not contacted promptly?
4. How often does the team miss a next action, and how do they know?
5. Which tools are already paid for?
6. What would make a pilot useful enough to pay for?
7. Who approves the workflow, data access, and budget?

Record answers accurately. Do not imply a prospect agreed to a problem or price unless they did.

## Funnel

1. Discovery: identify relevant businesses from legitimate public business information or referrals.
2. Qualification: confirm the business has the problem and a relevant decision-maker.
3. Personalized outreach: mention a specific, verifiable observation and ask permission to share a concise idea.
4. Conversation: understand current workflow, cost of friction, constraints, and success criteria.
5. Proposal: define scope, timeline, deliverables, exclusions, fee, and acceptance criteria.
6. Approval: obtain explicit agreement and required access before touching live systems or data.
7. Delivery: build and test with sample data first; document the actual result.
8. Payment: count revenue only when payment is verified received.
9. Learning: request permission to use an anonymized case study; never publish customer details without consent.

## Sample first-contact message

Subject: A small idea for your lead follow-up process

Hi [Name],

I noticed [specific, verifiable observation about the business]. I build practical AI and automation workflows, and I'm exploring whether a lightweight lead follow-up system could help teams keep track of enquiry status and the next action.

If lead follow-up is a current challenge, would a short, no-obligation conversation be useful? I can share a simple workflow example first—no access to your systems needed.

Best,
Aryan
SUPERPOWER AI

Personalize honestly. Respect opt-outs and applicable outreach rules. Do not send repeated unsolicited messages after a clear refusal.

## Evidence to track

- Prospects researched.
- Qualified prospects.
- Replies and conversations.
- Confirmed pain points.
- Proposals sent.
- Paid pilots agreed.
- Payments actually received.
- Delivery time and acceptance criteria met.
- Before/after process measurements, if the client agrees to measurement.

Use real counts and date ranges. A small sample is not proof of broad market demand.

## Pricing method

Do not select a price merely because it sounds premium. Estimate delivery effort, tool costs, support, risk, and the value the buyer confirms. Present a fixed pilot scope and price; state what is excluded. Validate willingness to pay through actual conversations and proposals.

## Ethical urgency

Allowed: “I have capacity for one more implementation this month” only if true.

Not allowed: fabricated customer demand, false deadlines, fake countdowns, fake discounts, or claims that the offer will never return when it will.

## First 14-day validation sprint

- Days 1–2: prepare the demo, sample data, screenshots, and short walkthrough.
- Days 3–5: interview a small batch of relevant prospects and log actual answers.
- Days 6–8: refine the offer around repeated, evidenced problems.
- Days 9–11: send tailored proposals only to prospects with a confirmed need.
- Days 12–14: review replies, objections, time required, and any paid commitment; decide whether to continue, change the niche, or stop.

The sprint is a plan, not a claim that these activities have already happened.
